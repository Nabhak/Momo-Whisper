Setup guide

How to install Momo Whisper

New to installing apps outside the App Store? No problem. Follow these six steps once and you'll be dictating in a couple of minutes.

1

Download and unzip

Click Download (right here, or the button top right — it always gives you the newest version). Your browser saves a .zip file to your Downloads folder. Double-click that file to unzip it, and you'll get an app called Momo Whisper.

Why a zip? It keeps the app in one tidy package so it downloads quickly and arrives intact.

2

Move it into Applications

Drag Momo Whisper into your Applications folder. Not sure where that is? Open a Finder window (the blue smiley face in your Dock) and you'll see Applications in the sidebar on the left.

Why here? Applications is where your Mac keeps all its apps and expects to find them, so everything runs smoothly.

3

Open it the first time

The first time you open Momo Whisper, macOS shows a warning like “Apple could not verify Momo Whisper is free of malware.” Don't worry, nothing is wrong. macOS says this about every free indie app that hasn't paid Apple's notarization fee. You only need to get past it once. Pick whichever way feels comfortable:

Option A · easiest, no typing
  1. In your Applications folder, right-click (or Control-click) Momo Whisper and choose Open.
  2. The warning appears again, but now with an Open button. Click Open.
  3. Don't see an Open button? Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to “Momo Whisper was blocked”, click Open Anyway, then enter your Mac password.
Option B · one Terminal command

Prefer a single command? Open Terminal (in Applications → Utilities), paste this, and press Return:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Momo Whisper.app"

It just removes the “downloaded from the internet” quarantine flag, nothing more.

This is a one-time step. After it opens once, Momo Whisper launches normally forever after.
4

Allow a few permissions

On first launch, Momo Whisper walks you through a few macOS permissions. Here's what each one is for:

  • Microphone, so it can hear you when you dictate.
  • Accessibility, so it can type the finished text into whatever app you're in.
  • Speech Recognition (recommended, optional), which turns on faster, more accurate on-device transcription; that audio stays on your Mac.

You can review or change these anytime in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

5

Add your free Groq key

Momo uses Groq to turn your speech into text and to translate it. It needs a Groq API key, which is like a free password that lets the app talk to Groq on your behalf.

It's free: create one at console.groq.com, copy it, and paste it into Momo's Settings. Your key is stored locally on your Mac, and Groq's free tier is generous for everyday dictation.

6

Start talking to your Mac

That's it! Hold Fn and speak to dictate, hold Fn⇧ to translate as you speak, or highlight any text and click the translate button. The menu-bar icon shows a live waveform while it listens.
