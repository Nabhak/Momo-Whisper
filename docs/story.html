The little tale behind Momo — why it exists, why your Mac frowns at it the first time, and how you can help. 🐾

It started with my own English 😅

I work at an international company, but English has never been my strong suit. A short Slack message or a quick email could eat half my morning — second-guessing every other word.

So I built a tiny menu-bar helper for myself: hold a key, speak in Thai, and clean English lands right where my cursor is. Highlight any text to translate it in place. Work suddenly felt a lot lighter.

Then I wanted to share it with friends

Turns out I wasn't the only one in that boat — friends kept asking to try it. So Momo grew from a personal little hack into something I'm happy to give away: free to use, built on top of the lovely open-source Whisper app by gamezxz. (Early builds went by Whisper Momo; same app, new name.)

Why your Mac shows a scary warning 🙈

The first time you open Momo, macOS says something like “Apple could not verify Momo is free of malware.” Nothing is wrong — it says that about every little indie app that hasn't paid Apple's toll.

To make that warning go away for good, a developer has to join the Apple Developer Program — $99 every year — so Apple can notarize the app. Momo is a free, one-person project, so it hasn't paid that yearly fee yet. The app is safe and its code is out in the open; you just click past the warning once (the install guide walks you through it).

One click the first time, then Momo opens normally forever after.
